## Q1. On a hiking map, which contour lines mark the steepest ground?

A. The thickest lines
B. The closest lines **correct**
C. The highest-numbered lines
D. The widest loops

NIV: 1 Samuel 14:13; context None
Paid hint: Think of the gaps between contours as the treads of a staircase.

Closely spaced contours show a large elevation change over a short horizontal distance. Jonathan's demanding ascent is a natural connection to steep terrain; the story does not describe a map. Line thickness marks index contours, and high elevation alone does not establish steepness.

## Q2. What can a sailor estimate from the North Star's height above the horizon?

A. Longitude
B. Compass error
C. Direction of travel
D. Latitude **correct**

NIV: Acts 27:20; context None
Paid hint: As you sail toward the North Pole, the star appears higher in the sky.

The North Star, Polaris, appears at an angle above the horizon approximately equal to the observer's northern latitude. In Acts, the loss of visible sun and stars supplies a connection to celestial navigation. The passage does not identify Polaris or the crew's method. Longitude, travel direction and compass error require other information.

## Q3. Which sea is bounded by ocean currents instead of coastlines?

A. Sargasso Sea **correct**
B. Mediterranean Sea
C. Caribbean Sea
D. Arabian Sea

NIV: Jonah 2:3-6; context None
Paid hint: Think of floating rafts of brown seaweed, living out on the open ocean.

The Sargasso Sea's boundaries are ocean currents. It is famous for floating sargassum seaweed. The seaweed in Jonah's prayer provides the association, but the story does not name that species or place Jonah in the Sargasso Sea. The passage is a prayer recalling distress and rescue.

## Q4. Which factor chiefly explains Kilimanjaro’s cold summit despite its tropical location?

A. Its elevation **correct**
B. Its longitude
C. Nearby ocean currents
D. Seasonal monsoon winds

NIV: Jeremiah 18:14; context JER.18.11-17
Paid hint: Climbers pass through several climate zones without travelling far north or south.

Temperature generally decreases with height in the troposphere. Kilimanjaro’s elevation creates cold summit conditions despite its tropical latitude. Longitude does not provide a comparable temperature control; ocean currents and monsoon winds do not explain the mountain’s vertical climate zones. Jeremiah contrasts reliable mountain snow and water with the people’s unfaithfulness; the passage is not a prediction about present-day glaciers.

## Q5. Summer flash floods in Zion can arrive under clear skies. What is their usual trigger?

A. Rapid mountain snowmelt
B. Rain falling farther upstream **correct**
C. A natural dam breaking
D. A surge from underground springs

NIV: 2 Kings 3:16-18; context None
Paid hint: Follow the valley beyond what you can see from where you are standing.

Zion's flash floods are commonly triggered by intense rain, sometimes miles upstream from a sunny canyon. Elisha's promise describes a valley receiving water despite no visible wind or rain. That contrast supports the modern inference that local skies need not reveal a valley's water source. It does not establish rainfall as the cause of the biblical event, which the passage presents as God's provision.
