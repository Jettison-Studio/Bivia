## Q1. Which motion chiefly causes the Sun’s apparent daily journey across our sky?

A. Earth orbiting the Sun
B. The Sun spinning on its axis
C. Earth spinning on its axis **correct**
D. The Moon orbiting Earth

NIV: Ecclesiastes 1:5; context ECC.1.2-11
Paid hint: Think about a day, rather than a year.

Earth's rotation produces the Sun's apparent daily motion. Ecclesiastes describes the recurring appearance from an observer's viewpoint as part of its reflection on cycles. The verse is not presenting an orbital model.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Four actual celestial motions. Day-versus-year clue distinguishes rotation from orbit. Reading supplies the daily cycle, but may add little to an already familiar astronomy question.

## Q2. What rising air do eagles often use to gain height without flapping?

A. Jet streams
B. Downdrafts
C. Sea breezes
D. Thermals **correct**

NIV: Isaiah 40:31; context ISA.40.25-31
Paid hint: Sun-warmed ground helps create these columns of air.

Thermals are rising columns of relatively warm air that soaring birds can use for lift. Isaiah uses soaring eagles as an image of renewed strength. The flight example illuminates the image without reducing its spiritual promise to an aerodynamic explanation.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Reading's soaring-with-renewed-strength image supports sustained flight with reduced flapping. Four real atmospheric motions. Hint provides the thermal source. Eagles also use other forms of lift; do not claim exclusivity. Coordinator rated as easy; rising excludes downdraft.

Sources: [source 1](https://academy.allaboutbirds.org/golden-eagle-soaring-on-a-thermal/), [source 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=azQJKhN1yNs)

## Q5. How many independent fair-coin tosses give exactly four equally likely ordered outcomes for choosing a starting player?

A. One
B. Two **correct**
C. Three
D. Four

NIV: Proverbs 18:18; context PRO.18.13-22
Paid hint: List the ordered head–tail sequences, keeping heads–tails separate from tails–heads.

Two independent fair tosses yield HH, HT, TH and TT, each with probability one-quarter. The proverb describes settling a dispute by lot; the coin scheme is a modern illustration of using a chance procedure, not a reconstruction of the ancient objects or method.

**unapproved:** Four plausible counting answers. Reading motivates selection by chance, but the mathematics stands independently. Long stem may be unnecessarily cumbersome; not a measured difficulty claim. Assign one sequence to each player before tossing.

## Q20. Alex and Sam support the ends of a light, horizontal pole at rest. A load hangs twice as far from Sam as from Alex. What share of its weight does Alex support?

B. One-half
C. Two-thirds **correct**
D. All of it

NIV: Numbers 13:23; context NUM.13.21-27
Paid hint: Balance the turning effects about Sam’s end of the pole.

With negligible pole weight, balancing torques about Sam gives Alex's force times the full span equal to the load's weight times two-thirds of that span. Alex therefore supports two-thirds. The scouts' carrying pole supplies the physical arrangement; the distances and names are an invented modern problem, not biblical measurements.

**unapproved:** Four plausible load-share estimates. Explicit static/light-pole assumptions. More calculation-heavy than other items; retain for later band only if playtest finds it enjoyable. Same general torque field as cleared Fitness dumbbell item, different geometry and inference. Hint identifies a useful pivot without supplying fraction.
