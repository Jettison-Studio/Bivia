## Q1. What creates the floating figure in the classic Pepper’s Ghost stage illusion?

A. An image projected onto drifting mist
B. A hidden figure reflected in angled glass **correct**
C. A costume glowing under ultraviolet light

NIV: 1 Corinthians 13:12; context 1CO.13.8-13
Paid hint: The audience can look through the same surface that reflects another image.

Mist projection, ultraviolet effects and rotating scenery are other theatrical techniques. Paul contrasts a present reflected view with future face-to-face knowledge; the reflection detail supports the optical connection without turning his point about incomplete knowledge into an illusion claim.

## Q4. Which theatrical device best fits staging the rich man’s deliberation in verses 17–19?

A. A dialogue
B. A soliloquy **correct**
C. A choral response
D. Third-person narration

NIV: Luke 12:16–21; context LUK.12.13-21
Paid hint: Hamlet’s “To be, or not to be” is a famous example.

A soliloquy gives an audience access to a character’s own thoughts through speech. The rich man works through his plans by addressing himself, which fits that device in a proposed stage adaptation. Dialogue exchanges speech between characters; a choral response comes from a group; third-person narration describes a character from outside. The surrounding parable challenges confidence in stored wealth; it is not advice endorsing his plans.

Exclude the overlapping broader term monologue and the potentially ambiguous aside. The Hamlet example is an independent clue whose benefit is unmeasured.
