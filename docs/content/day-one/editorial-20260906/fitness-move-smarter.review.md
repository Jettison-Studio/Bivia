## Q3. Holding the same dumbbell at shoulder height, which position creates the least turning effect about the shoulder?

A. Arm straight forward, palm down
B. Elbow bent, weight close to the shoulder **correct**
C. Arm straight out to the side, palm down
D. Arm straight forward, palm up

NIV: Luke 15:5; context LUK.15.3-7
Paid hint: Compare the horizontal distance between the weight and the joint.

The dumbbell produces the smallest gravitational torque when its horizontal distance from the shoulder is smallest. Its weight has not changed. Turning the palm does not change the dumbbell’s horizontal lever arm when the arm position stays the same. The shepherd places the sheep on his shoulders rather than at arm’s length; this supplies the concrete load-placement analogy. Jesus explains joy over repentance, not an exercise prescription.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Replaces the impossible mass/air distractors with achievable holding positions. All weights are at shoulder height; bringing the weight close reduces its horizontal lever arm. Palm orientation is a plausible but irrelevant variable for the weight’s gravitational torque. Tests external dumbbell torque only, not the total muscular effort including arm weight.

## Q5. Which gym exercise most closely matches lifting yourself toward a branch during a climb?

A. A bench press
B. An overhead press
C. A triceps dip
D. A pull-up **correct**

NIV: Luke 19:3–4; context LUK.19.1-10
Paid hint: Your hands stay overhead while your elbows bend.

A pull-up lifts the body toward hands fixed overhead, resembling one arm action that can occur when climbing a tree. Bench presses, overhead presses, and triceps dips primarily use pushing actions. The passage supplies Zacchaeus’s climb to see Jesus; it does not describe his hand placement or prescribe a workout. Its wider focus is his encounter with Jesus and changed conduct.

**unapproved_repair:** Replaces held resistance-band/bronze-bow pairing. All four choices are real upper-body exercises, so lower-body options do not give the answer away. The branch maneuver is an explicit illustrative part of climbing, not a claim about Zacchaeus’s exact technique. Easy movement analogy. Reading contribution remains a review risk because the stem supplies the branch-climbing situation; test with full reading rather than treat as approved. Clue distinguishes fixed overhead hands from pressing a weight overhead, with no measured benefit claim. Do not claim pull-ups reproduce all climbing movements or exclude leg work.
