## Q3. What material was used for the traditional victory wreath at the ancient Olympic Games?

A. Fresh laurel branches
B. Gold shaped into olive leaves
C. Wool woven into a headband
D. Fresh olive branches **correct**

NIV: 1 Corinthians 9:24-25; context 1CO.9.19-27
Paid hint: The tree also supplied a staple cooking oil.

Ancient Olympic victors traditionally received a wreath of olive branches. The perishable athletic crown in Paul’s comparison supports a plant-material inference, while the hint distinguishes olive from laurel. Paul is contrasting athletic discipline and temporary reward with enduring gospel purpose; he does not identify a particular festival or plant here. Do not claim all Greek games used olive.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Laurel is a plausible confusion with other classical honors; gold olive leaves are a plausible confusion with durable ceremonial wreaths. Wool is a textile headband alternative. Full reading narrows material but does not choose olive over laurel. No claim Paul specifically describes the Olympics.

## Q4. Which sailing manoeuvre switches between angled courses to make progress upwind?

A. Gybing
B. Heaving to
C. Tacking **correct**
D. Bearing away

NIV: Acts 27:7-8; context ACT.27.4-10
Paid hint: The bow turns through the wind as the boat changes sides.

Beating upwind uses successive close-hauled courses joined by tacks. Gybing turns the stern through the wind; heaving to reduces progress; bearing away turns farther from the wind. Acts describes adverse wind forcing a change of route, supporting the need to work around a wind constraint. It does not state that the ancient vessel used the modern racing manoeuvre described here.

**unapproved:** Specific adverse-wind connection, but the reading does not teach the manoeuvre name. All choices are real sailing actions. Paid hint distinguishes tacking from gybing; terminology familiarity requires testing.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.rya.org.uk/training/do-you-know-your-points-of-sail/)
