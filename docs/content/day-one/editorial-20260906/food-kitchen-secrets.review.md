## Q1. Which nutrient supplies most of the food energy in raisins?

A. Fat
B. Protein
C. Carbohydrate **correct**
D. Minerals

NIV: 1 Samuel 30:11-12; context 1SA.30.9-15
Paid hint: Think of the natural sweetness concentrated in a dried grape.

Raisins contain much more carbohydrate, including natural sugars, than protein or fat. Minerals do not provide food energy. The passage describes feeding and giving water to an abandoned, hungry man who revives. This supports a food-energy connection; it does not isolate raisins as the sole cause of recovery or prescribe treatment for starvation.

Sweetness helps connect sugars to carbohydrates; review whether the paid hint adds value at this difficulty. Nutrition table is an industry brochure hosted by USDA, not an independent USDA laboratory measurement.

## Q2. When cream is churned into butter, what mainly gathers into clumps?

A. Milk proteins
B. Sugar crystals
C. Fat droplets **correct**
D. Air bubbles

NIV: Proverbs 30:33; context PRO.30.29-33
Paid hint: Think of the greasy mark a pat of butter leaves on paper.

Churning disrupts membranes around fat droplets so they join into butter grains. Proteins surround the droplets but are not the main material forming butter; sugar remains dissolved, and whipped-in air is lost as butter forms. Proverbs uses an everyday cause-and-effect comparison to warn against provoking conflict.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** The verse names butter but not the component. Paid clue adds an observable property; test whether it earns its cost.

Sources: [source 1](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/icooks/11-17-03.html)

## Q3. Which habit helps keep Turkish coffee’s sediment near the bottom of the cup?

A. Swirling the cup before each sip
B. Letting the cup stand briefly **correct**
C. Pouring it between two cups
D. Stirring upward from the bottom

NIV: Jeremiah 48:11; context JER.48.7-15
Paid hint: Gravity can do the separating while the liquid stays still.

Fine grounds remain in unfiltered Turkish coffee and settle near the bottom when the cup rests. Swirling, pouring between cups and stirring from the bottom tend to disturb this sediment. Some fine particles remain suspended; the claim is not complete clarification. Jeremiah compares Moab’s undisturbed ease to wine resting on dregs before announcing judgment, not to coffee drinking.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Replaces implausible chemistry distractors with ordinary handling actions. Rest allows settling; agitation tends to resuspend grounds. New operational stem is intentionally easy after the wine-dregs reading; paid hint may be unnecessary, so its benefit remains unproven. Accessible producer source resolves earlier PDF-fetch dependency.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.mehmetefendi.com/eng/turkish-coffee/characteristics), [source 2](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/turkish-coffee-culture-and-tradition-00645)

## Q4. What creates much of a bread crust’s brown color and toasted aroma?

A. Starch absorbing water
B. Yeast releasing carbon dioxide
C. Fat trapping air bubbles
D. Sugars reacting with proteins **correct**

NIV: Ruth 2:14; context RUT.2.8-16
Paid hint: Toast and seared meat share this browning process.

Maillard reactions between reducing sugars and amino groups contribute strongly to bread crust color and aroma. Water absorption, fermentation gas and air trapping affect other baking properties. Boaz shares bread and roasted grain with Ruth in a scene of welcome and provision. Roasting supplies the food-transformation connection without naming the modern reaction.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Replaces the held grain-legume nutrition question. Does not ask the reaction’s technical name. Hint supplies a second browned-food comparison. Other browning chemistry can contribute, so the stem says much of. Pairing remains subject to coordinator judgment; no claim Ruth teaches chemistry.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2022/02/15/understanding-the-maillard-reaction-in-baking), [source 2](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/meat/activity-maillard.html)

## Q5. Which gas builds pressure as grape juice ferments into wine?

A. Oxygen
B. Nitrogen
C. Carbon dioxide **correct**
D. Methane

NIV: Matthew 9:17; context MAT.9.14-17
Paid hint: It is also the gas that makes a sparkling drink fizz.

Alcoholic fermentation converts sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide. The other gases are not the gas produced in this reaction. The bursting wineskin is a concrete pressure connection; the verse does not name the gas. Jesus gives the image while answering a question about fasting.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Fizz clue adds another familiar observation but still requires gas identification. Test incremental value; the Bible reading itself only supplies pressure imagery.

Sources: [source 1](https://archive.progress.oregonstate.edu/fall-2011/fermentation-it%E2%80%99s-culture), [source 2](https://owri.oregonstate.edu/sites/agscid7/files/owri_report.pdf)
