## Q7. If a painter mixed the two contrasting colors in this verse, which color-mixing term would describe the result?

A. A shade
B. A secondary color
C. A tint **correct**
D. A tone

NIV: Isaiah 1:18; context ISA.1.15-20
Paid hint: Pastel versions of colors belong to this group.

The verse contrasts red with white. Mixing a colored paint with white makes a tint in standard painting terminology. Adding black makes a shade; adding gray makes a tone; combining two primary colors makes a secondary color in traditional painting instruction. The proposed mixing is a modern activity using the imagery, not the process by which the passage says sins become clean.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Replaces held wick/capillarity question and removes one of two candle scenarios. Reading supplies the colors instead of the stem naming them. Four real color-mixing labels, with the secondary-color distractor targeting the misconception that mixing any two paints creates a secondary color. Original pastel clue remains unmeasured. This is an easy art-vocabulary item; Entertainment category uses leisure painting, requiring taxonomy review. Does not claim red plus white produces pure white, or explain forgiveness physically. No recommendation to fit the 2.5-second timer tail.

## Q10. For the same object still floating at rest, what increases when the water becomes less dense?

A. The upward buoyant force
B. The volume of water displaced **correct**
C. The object’s mass
D. The object’s weight

NIV: Ezekiel 47:8–9; context EZK.47.6-12
Paid hint: The water still has to support the same weight.

A floating object displaces its own weight of water. When water density falls, a greater displaced volume is needed to provide that same supporting force. Ezekiel’s vision supplies a salt-to-fresh contrast; this is a separate modern floating-object inference, not an explanation of the vision’s miraculous life-giving river.

**coordinator_timing_hold:** Replaces salt-word giveaway with a further density-to-displacement inference. Four physical quantities; mass and weight are distinct but correlated distractors. Assume the object remains afloat, its mass is unchanged, and local gravity is unchanged. Substantially harder than v1; unsuitable for a 2.5-second tail without a timer change. Coordinator: conceptually valid next-step proposal, but timing/placement-sensitive and not test-ready; 30-second estimate incompatible with tail.
