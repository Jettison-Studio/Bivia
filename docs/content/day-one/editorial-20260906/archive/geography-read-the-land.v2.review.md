## 1. On a hiking map, which contour lines mark the steepest ground?

A. The thickest lines  
B. The closest lines **(correct)**  
C. The highest-numbered lines  
D. The widest loops  

**Full reading:** 1 Samuel 14:13 (NIV, fetched at runtime).

**Original paid hint:** Think of the gaps between contours as the treads of a staircase.

**Fact and every option.** [USGS map symbols](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/TopographicMapSymbols/topomapsymbols.pdf), page 1, establishes the relationship between contour spacing and slope. Close lines mean rapid elevation change over a short horizontal distance. Thick lines are index contours; high numbers indicate elevation, not steepness; the extent of a closed loop alone cannot establish slope. This compares ordinary contours on one map, not maps with different scales or intervals. The fact was retrieved in the first pass; this pass's repeat PDF open failed, so no new retrieval is claimed for that URL.

**Context and full-reading leakage.** [1 Samuel 14 in the independent WEB text](https://ebible.org/eng-web/1SA14.htm) places Jonathan's ascent in the rocky pass during his attack on the outpost. His climbing detail points to steep ground, but neither the exact NIV reading nor the surrounding ten verses mentions contours or map spacing. The verse also contains combat; it has not been sanitized or rewritten. The hint supplies a visual way to compare horizontal steps with climbing, without saying that the correct lines are close together.

**Playtest focus.** An accessible opener. Check that “treads” is understood; the hint should create a picture, not introduce another term the player must decode. The full reading alone still requires map knowledge.

## 2. What can a sailor estimate from the North Star's height above the horizon?

A. Longitude  
B. Compass error  
C. Direction of travel  
D. Latitude **(correct)**  

**Full reading:** Acts 27:20 (NIV, fetched at runtime).

**Original paid hint:** As you sail toward the North Pole, the star appears higher in the sky.

**Fact and every option.** [NOAA's navigation history](https://vos.noaa.gov/MWL/aug_08/navigation_tools.shtml) connects the North Star's elevation to latitude and distinguishes the longitude problem. The estimate is approximate and applies where Polaris is visible, in the Northern Hemisphere. “Height above the horizon” means angular height, not distance to the star. Longitude needs other information; direction of travel cannot be inferred from a stationary angular observation. [NOAA's navigation discussion](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/world-magnetic-model) distinguishes position from direction of travel. “Compass error” is the accessible distractor label: an altitude measurement alone contains no compass bearing to compare. [NOAA's magnetic-declination explanation](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/magnetic-declination) documents one relevant compass/true-north difference. All four choices are navigational information, with no unit conversion or exact measurement required.

**Context and full-reading leakage.** [Acts 27:18–26 in the independent KJV text](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+27:18-26&version=KJV) includes the prolonged storm, obscured sky and subsequent encouragement from Paul. Loss of celestial reference points supplies the connection; the account does not identify Polaris or the method used by the crew. The exact NIV verse names none of the four answers. The paid hint adds a directional thought experiment that still requires the player to connect north–south position to latitude.

**Playtest focus.** Is the distinction between latitude and longitude satisfying rather than school-test recall? The stem is now one short sentence, and technical terms “magnetic declination” and “course over ground” are gone.

## 3. Which sea is bounded by ocean currents instead of coastlines?

A. Sargasso Sea **(correct)**  
B. Mediterranean Sea  
C. Caribbean Sea  
D. Arabian Sea  

**Full reading:** Jonah 2:3-6 (NIV, fetched at runtime).

**Original paid hint:** Think of floating rafts of brown seaweed, living out on the open ocean.

**Fact and every option.** [NOAA's Sargasso Sea explanation](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/sargassosea.html) explicitly identifies current-defined boundaries and floating sargassum. [NOAA Ocean Exploration](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/expedition-feature/okeanos-ex2104-features-sargasso-sea/) independently describes the floating algae habitat. The distractors are genuine seas with coastal boundaries: NOAA's sea-name records identify coasts for the [Mediterranean's western basin](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/archive/archive-management-system/OAS/bin/prd/jquery/seaname/details/116), [Caribbean](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/archive/archive-management-system/OAS/bin/prd/jquery/seaname/details/50) and [Arabian Sea](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/archive/archive-management-system/OAS/bin/prd/jquery/seaname/details/36). No distractor was selected merely because its name sounded plausible. The question concerns the sea's boundaries; it does not say there are no islands within it.

**Context and full-reading leakage.** [Jonah 2 in the independent WEB text](https://ebible.org/eng-web/JON02.htm) is a prayer from inside the fish recalling distress and rescue. The exact four-verse NIV reading includes the seaweed detail and the turn toward rescue; the whole chapter was retrieved for context. It names no sea or algae species. The connection is a concrete seaweed association, not a claim that Jonah traveled to the Sargasso Sea or encountered sargassum. The paid hint adds the free-floating, open-ocean form of the algae without supplying the answer's name or its etymology. Seaweed exists in the distractor seas too, so the hint is a nudge, not independent proof of uniqueness; the boundary fact determines the answer.

**Playtest focus.** This may be the hardest fact in the round. Check whether the floating-seaweed clue helps a non-specialist retrieve Sargasso, or merely feels like another fact they do not know. It is a genuine replacement for the failed cloud-forest slot, with four credible same-type options.

## 4. How did floods help form the round rock potholes at Minnesota's Interstate State Park?

A. Salt crystals expanding in cracks  
B. Tree roots splitting the rock  
C. Sand and pebbles swirling in the water **(correct)**  
D. Ice repeatedly freezing inside cracks  

**Full reading:** Job 14:18-20 (NIV, fetched at runtime).

**Original paid hint:** Picture the inside of a stone mortar after years of use.

**Fact and every option.** [Minnesota DNR's geological-wonders feature](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/assets/mcv/2024/may-jun/yn/YN-7Wonders.pdf), final PDF page, documents sand and pebbles swirling during floods and wearing the park's rock potholes. The specific park avoids generalizing to unrelated depressions called potholes. [NPS weathering](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/weathering-erosion.htm) supports salt and frost wedging; [NPS physical weathering](https://home.nps.gov/subjects/erosion/weathering.htm) includes root expansion. A, B and D are real rock-breaking processes, but not the site's documented flood-grinding mechanism. The mortar is an original illustrative comparison, not a claim from the biblical account or a quotation from DNR.

**Context and full-reading leakage.** [Job 14:14–22 in the independent KJV text](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Job+14:14-22&version=KJV) establishes Job's lament about mortality and destroyed hope. The exact NIV range retains that emotional comparison. Water wearing rock is already in the reading; the additional knowledge is how sediment can do the grinding. The new hint contains no sand, pebbles, carried material, circling water, or wording from the correct option. A familiar grinding vessel invites an analogy about wear without narrating the answer mechanism.

**Playtest focus.** Check both ends: does the mortar image help, and does it still leave a worthwhile inference? Someone unfamiliar with a mortar and pestle may find this hint less useful. Do not expand it back into the old explanation about floodwater carrying hard tools.

## 5. Summer flash floods in Zion can arrive under clear skies. What is their usual trigger?

A. Rapid mountain snowmelt  
B. Rain falling farther upstream **(correct)**  
C. A natural dam breaking  
D. A surge from underground springs  

**Full reading:** 2 Kings 3:16-18 (NIV, fetched at runtime).

**Original paid hint:** Follow the valley beyond what you can see from where you are standing.

**Fact and every option.** [Zion's NPS flash-flood guidance](https://home.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/flash-flood.htm) describes intense rainfall, storms miles away and sunny skies over the affected canyon. The [NPS wilderness guide](https://www.nps.gov/zion/learn/news/upload/Zion_Wilderness_Guide_2021web.pdf) distinguishes spring snowmelt, typically March–early June, from flash floods more common in mid-summer and early fall. A is a real source of high water, but not the usual summer clear-sky flash-flood trigger described here. [National Weather Service flood hazards](https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-hazards) confirms that dam failures can cause flash floods, making C plausible without making it the usual explanation at Zion. [NPS identifies natural springs as groundwater outlets](https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/watersources.htm); their existence does not establish a spring surge as Zion's usual flash-flood trigger. B is the supported best answer. **Keep “summer,” “Zion” and “usual”:** removing those qualifiers would invite valid alternative flood causes. This question does not diagnose a particular real flood or instruct a player to wait and identify its cause.

**Context and full-reading leakage.** [2 Kings 3 in the independent WEB text](https://ebible.org/eng-web/2KI03.htm) records an army without water, Elisha's promise and water arriving from the direction of Edom. The exact NIV reading says the valley will receive water despite no visible wind or rain and presents that as God's provision. It does not state that rain fell upstream. The useful parallel is the mismatch between what an observer sees overhead and water arriving in a valley. The question asks about an ordinary modern mechanism; it does not explain away the biblical event. WEB and NIV render verse 16 differently (trenches versus pools); neither the question nor hint relies on that difference. Only the retrieved NIV will be used for the player.

**Playtest focus.** The paid hint adds the spatial step of following the valley out of sight; it does not name rain or upstream runoff. Check that this feels more illuminating than the discarded property-rights/wells analogy. The reading's divine-provision context must remain intact in preview and any discussion.
