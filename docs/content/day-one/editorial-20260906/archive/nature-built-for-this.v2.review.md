## Q1: credible hoof alternatives

What gives mountain goats extra grip on bare rock?

A. Hard, smooth soles  
B. Soft pads inside hard hoof rims **correct**  
C. Deep grooves in the hoof walls  
D. Long toes that dig into rock

NIV: Psalm 104:18. Hint retained: Picture the sole of a climbing shoe.

[NPS](https://www.nps.gov/noca/learn/nature/mountain-goats.htm) supports pliable split hooves with soft pads and hard outer lining. The alternatives now concern hoof construction, rather than suction cups, adhesive hairs, or retractable claws. They need a credibility check; the author does not certify the revision as resolved. The hard rim is only part of the combined grip mechanism. The biblical animal is not identified as a North American mountain goat.

## Q5: infer a consequence

What do aspen stems grown from one tree’s roots usually share?

A. The same age  
B. The same height  
C. The same lifespan  
D. The same genetic makeup **correct**

NIV: Isaiah 11:1–3. New hint: This growth skips pollen and seeds.

[Forest Service silvics](https://research.fs.usda.gov/silvics/quaking-aspen) describes the clone as a multistemmed individual. Its [species review](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/poptre) documents variable sprout development and survival. Root sprouting no longer appears among the answers: the player must infer its genetic consequence. Differently aged stems and local growing conditions defeat the other options. The hint adds absence of sexual reproduction; it does not repeat a word in the correct answer. Test its incremental value and whether the revised stem is too easy. Isaiah remains Davidic imagery, not a claim about aspen species. Do not claim all groves are one clone or that mutation never occurs.
