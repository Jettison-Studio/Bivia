## Q1. How does a hot griddle mainly transfer heat into dough touching its surface?

A. Convection
B. Conduction **correct**
C. Radiation
D. Evaporation

NIV: Leviticus 2:5; context LEV.2.4-9.
Paid clue: Focus on the place where the dough meets the cooking surface.

Heat passes by conduction across the contact between a hot cooking surface and dough. The reading describes a grain offering prepared on a griddle; it supplies a concrete example of contact cooking, not a physics explanation.

Easy terminology transfer. Hint directs attention to contact but may add little for players who already know conduction; benefit unmeasured. Rejected 1 Kings 19:6 pairing because NIV specifies coals, not a baking stone.

Sources: [source](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2021/10/04/how-to-turn-any-home-oven-into-a-pizza-oven)

## Q2. Why do you see distant lightning before hearing its thunder?

A. Thunder begins several seconds after the flash
B. The light has a shorter distance to travel
C. Light travels much faster than sound **correct**
D. Your ears take several seconds to detect sound

NIV: Job 37:3–4; context JOB.37.1-8.
Paid clue: The flash and thunder originate in the same lightning event.

Light reaches an observer far faster than the sound of thunder. Elihu's speech places the flash before the roar, offering an observable sequence; it does not state a measured speed of either wave.

Easy causal explanation with competing timing, distance and perception hypotheses. Hint weakens the delayed-origin option. Adjacent passage reused from held Entertainment thunder-sheet question; different concept, but avoid both in a final session.

Sources: [source](https://www.weather.gov/fgz/Lightning)

## Q3. In traditional winnowing, what carries the loose chaff away from the grain?

A. Moving air **correct**
B. Flowing water
C. A mesh screen
D. Rotating grindstones

NIV: Luke 3:17; context LUK.3.15-20.
Paid clue: The lighter pieces travel farther from where the mixture falls.

Winnowing uses moving air to separate lighter chaff from heavier grain. John's image distinguishes gathering grain from disposing of chaff; burning follows separation and is not the separating mechanism. The surrounding passage concerns judgment, not instructions for farming.

All choices are real processing mechanisms. Reading names the operation but does not name the answer; hint supplies weight contrast. Familiarity with winnowing still affects difficulty.

Sources: [source](https://www.fao.org/4/x0039e/X0039E06.htm)

## Q4. Which place would best protect old family letters?

A. A sunny windowsill
B. A damp cellar
C. A hot attic
D. A cool, dry, dark cupboard **correct**

NIV: Jeremiah 32:14; context JER.32.9-15.
Paid clue: Avoid moisture and large swings in temperature.

Stable, cool, relatively dry storage away from light helps preserve paper. Jeremiah directs that purchase documents be placed in a clay jar for long keeping. The shared idea is protective storage; a modern letter need not be sealed in an ancient-style jar.

Intentionally easy guest choice among real household locations. Passage makes preservation purpose explicit; modern environmental conditions require outside knowledge. Hint may largely confirm the obvious choice; measure before approval.

Sources: [source](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/care/paper.html)

## Q5. Which device turns the trees into speaking characters in Jotham’s story?

A. Alliteration
B. Flashback
C. Personification **correct**
D. Onomatopoeia

NIV: Judges 9:8–15; context JDG.9.7-16.
Paid clue: It also lets a cartoon teapot argue with its owner.

Personification gives nonhuman things human qualities or actions. Jotham makes trees speak and choose a ruler in a warning about kingship. The trees are characters in his story, not a claim about plant behavior.

Replaces held fish question. Reading supplies the actual example; hint offers a second familiar example. Easy literary terminology with four real devices. Eight-verse display may be long for an easy guest item; assess reading burden. Full reading contains no answer term.

Sources: [source](https://poets.org/glossary/personification)
