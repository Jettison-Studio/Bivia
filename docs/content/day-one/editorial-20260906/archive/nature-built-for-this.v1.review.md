## 2. Turkey vultures are unusually good at finding food with which sense?

D. Smell **(correct)**  

**NIV reading:** Luke 17:37

**Original paid hint:** Their next meal may be hidden beneath a thick canopy of leaves.

[Cornell's Turkey Vulture guide](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Turkey_Vulture/) documents locating carcasses by smell beneath forest canopy. [Cornell's explanatory comparison](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/news/do-vultures-find-dead-animals-by-smell-or-by-tracking-predators-or-scavengers-on-the-ground/) distinguishes turkey vultures from visually oriented black vultures. D is correct. Sight is a real additional sense and the strongest distractor, so the question says “unusually good” rather than claiming they use smell exclusively. Hearing and touch are not the documented special ability. Do not generalize this to every vulture species.

No disputed eschatological interpretation is needed for the association. Neither the verse nor surrounding NIV range identifies an American species or mentions smell. The paid clue supplies concealment under leaves: the player must connect an unseen, silent food source with airborne odors. This is a specific food-location connection, not a claim that the biblical vultures were turkey vultures.

The publisher’s authored biology text supports these roles: [cellulose versus storage carbohydrates](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/3-2-carbohydrates), [chlorophyll as a light-absorbing pigment](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/8-1-overview-of-photosynthesis), and [nitrate in nitrogen nutrition](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/22-3-prokaryotic-metabolism). ## 4. Which cue helps many migrating birds sense autumn, even while the weather stays warm?

A. A full moon  
B. The first frost  
C. Shortening daylight **(correct)**  
D. A drop in air pressure  

**NIV reading:** Jeremiah 8:7

**Original paid hint:** The cue follows Earth's yearly journey around the Sun.

[Cornell's migration overview](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/news/the-basics-how-why-and-where-of-bird-migration/) identifies changing day length as an important trigger, alongside weather, food and innate timing. C fits a seasonal signal that persists during unusually warm weather. Frost is temperature-dependent; a pressure change is short-term weather; full moons repeat monthly and are not an annual autumn signal. Do not claim all species follow one cue or that weather cannot affect departure. [NASA's seasons explanation](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en/) supports the hint's annual astronomical connection: axial tilt as Earth orbits changes daylight duration.

[Jeremiah 8's public-domain context](https://ebible.org/eng-web/JER08.htm) contrasts birds observing their times with the people's failure to heed God. It is a rebuke, not a pleasant devotional about travel. The full NIV verse gives migration timing but not its mechanism. The orbital hint adds a stable annual reference without using “daylight” or saying that this is Jeremiah's scientific explanation. Research translations use different bird names; the question does not depend on that taxonomic difference.

## 5. How can one quaking aspen produce a grove of genetic copies?

A. Seeds from the same parent  
B. New shoots from its roots **(correct)**  
C. Pollen from the same tree  
D. Fungal links between trunks  

**NIV reading:** Isaiah 11:1-3

**Original paid hint:** A new stem can begin without a flower ever being pollinated.

[The Forest Service's aspen silvics account](https://research.fs.usda.gov/silvics/quaking-aspen) establishes vegetative root sprouting and the clone concept. [Its species review](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/poptre) distinguishes seed regeneration from root sprouts. B produces clonal stems. Seeds from one parent and pollen from one tree do not by themselves guarantee genetically identical offspring; fungal links are not plant cloning. The question does not claim every grove is one clone, all roots remain connected forever, or every cell remains mutation-free. Mature quaking aspens generally sprout from roots, so the answer intentionally avoids calling their typical process stump sprouting.

[Isaiah 11:1–10 in public-domain KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+11:1-10&version=KJV) supplies independent context after the WEB page failed to load. The retrieved NIV reading includes the coming ruler's qualities after the opening plant image. Its new growth from Jesse's stump/roots is royal imagery, not an aspen identification. Root/shoot language makes B attractive, but genetic copying still requires distinguishing vegetative growth from seed reproduction. The paid clue adds absence of pollination. Full-reading difficulty may fall below the author's estimate; test this explicitly and hold it if the reading functions as a semantic giveaway.
