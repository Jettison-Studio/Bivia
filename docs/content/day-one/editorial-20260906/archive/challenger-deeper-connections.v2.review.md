## Q1. Which motion chiefly causes the Sun’s apparent daily journey across our sky?

A. Earth orbiting the Sun
B. The Sun spinning on its axis
C. Earth spinning on its axis **correct**
D. The Moon orbiting Earth

NIV: Ecclesiastes 1:5; context ECC.1.2-11.
Paid clue: Think about a day, rather than a year.

Earth's rotation produces the Sun's apparent daily motion. Ecclesiastes describes the recurring appearance from an observer's viewpoint as part of its reflection on cycles. The verse is not presenting an orbital model.

unapproved: Four actual celestial motions. Day-versus-year clue distinguishes rotation from orbit. Reading supplies the daily cycle, but may add little to an already familiar astronomy question.

## Q2. Which stance gives the widest side-to-side base of support?

A. Feet side by side, shoulder-width apart **correct**
B. Feet touching side by side
C. One foot directly in front of the other
D. Standing on one foot

NIV: Psalm 18:36; context PSA.18.32-39.
Paid clue: Imagine the outline around all the points where your feet touch the floor.

A wider lateral separation of the feet enlarges the side-to-side base of support. The psalm associates a broad path with secure footing. This is an analogy between room for stable placement and stance geometry, not a promise that one stance prevents every fall.

## Q3. What rising air do eagles often use to gain height without flapping?

A. Jet streams
B. Downdrafts
C. Sea breezes
D. Thermals **correct**

NIV: Isaiah 40:31; context ISA.40.25-31.
Paid clue: Sun-warmed ground helps create these columns of air.

Thermals are rising columns of relatively warm air that soaring birds can use for lift. Isaiah uses soaring eagles as an image of renewed strength. The flight example illuminates the image without reducing its spiritual promise to an aerodynamic explanation.

unapproved: Reading's soaring-with-renewed-strength image supports sustained flight with reduced flapping. Four real atmospheric motions. Hint provides the thermal source. Eagles also use other forms of lift; do not claim exclusivity.

Sources: [source](https://academy.allaboutbirds.org/golden-eagle-soaring-on-a-thermal/), [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=azQJKhN1yNs)

## Q4. Which woodworker’s machine, like a potter’s wheel, rotates the work being shaped?

A. A bandsaw
B. A lathe **correct**
C. A drill press
D. A jointer

NIV: Jeremiah 18:3–4; context JER.18.1-10.
Paid clue: It can turn a square wood blank into a round chair leg.

A wood lathe holds and rotates the workpiece while cutting tools shape it. A potter's wheel also rotates the material being shaped, though clay is formed differently. Jeremiah's lesson concerns the potter's authority to reshape, not a historical origin claim about lathes.

unapproved: Four real workshop machines; distinguish rotating workpiece from rotating drill or cutter. Hint adds a familiar turned product. Pottery-wheel operation requires common outside knowledge beyond the text.

## Q5. Which biological feature best explains the locusts’ cold-to-sunshine change in activity?

A. Metamorphosis
B. Photoperiodism
C. Ectothermy **correct**
D. Diapause

NIV: Nahum 3:17; context NAM.3.14-19.
Paid clue: Focus on body temperature, rather than life stage or seasonal day length.

Locust activity is sensitive to body temperature, and these ectotherms use environmental warmth and behavior to regulate it. Nahum compares unreliable officials with locusts that settle in cold and depart in sunshine. It is a judgment image built around observed behavior, not a claim that every sunny moment triggers flight.

unapproved: All four terms describe real aspects of insect biology. Specific cold/sunshine contrast supports the inference. Avoid saying insects generate no metabolic heat or are passive thermometers. Hint narrows competing life-cycle interpretations.

Sources: [source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3145186/), [source](https://www.fao.org/ag/locusts/common/ecg/347_en_DLG1e.pdf)

## Q8. Before grain is winnowed, what is threshing meant to do?

A. Free kernels from the harvested stalks **correct**
B. Blow away loose chaff
C. Grind kernels into flour
D. Separate bran from flour

NIV: Deuteronomy 25:4; context DEU.25.1-10.
Paid clue: An animal’s hooves can do this job without turning grain into flour.

Threshing releases grain from the plant structures to which it is attached. Winnowing then uses air movement to separate loose lighter material. The command concerns allowing a working ox to eat; its treading image supplies a concrete threshing action.

unapproved: All options are genuine grain-processing tasks. Related to guest winnowing but asks a distinct preceding operation, explicitly tracked. Hint rules against flour grinding but partly repeats treading clue. Do not place both questions back-to-back across guest/category play.

Sources: [source](https://www.fao.org/4/x0039e/X0039E06.htm), [source](https://www.fao.org/4/t0522e/T0522E07.htm)

## Q9. Which optical behavior forms a clear face image in still water?

A. Diffuse reflection
B. Specular reflection **correct**
C. Refraction
D. Diffraction

NIV: Proverbs 27:19; context PRO.27.17-24.
Paid clue: A smooth surface sends neighboring reflected rays in orderly directions.

Specular reflection from a smooth surface preserves the directional relationships needed for a recognizable reflected image. Rough surfaces scatter reflected light across differing directions. The proverb compares a face reflected in water with the relationship between life and heart; it does not distinguish optical terminology.

unapproved: Reading supplies a recognizable image in water; four real optical processes. Related to earlier Pepper’s Ghost optics, but different question and passage. Hint adds smooth-surface behavior; technical vocabulary may dominate difficulty.

## Q10. A pear branch is grafted onto quince rootstock. What fruit should that branch produce?

A. Quinces
B. A pear–quince hybrid fruit
C. Pears **correct**
D. Pears one year and quinces the next

NIV: Romans 11:17–18; context ROM.11.16-24.
Paid clue: Grafting joins the plants without crossing their reproductive cells.

A grafted pear scion retains its fruit identity on compatible quince rootstock. The rootstock can influence vigor and other characteristics without turning that branch into a sexual hybrid. Paul’s olive-tree analogy emphasizes the supporting root and humility; it is not a promise about fruit traits.

unapproved: Four plausible novice models for joined plants. Question is restricted to the pear branch, excluding rootstock suckers that might bear different fruit. Hint distinguishes grafting from breeding. Root-supported branch idea is relevant, but text does not explicitly settle fruit identity. Some pear cultivars require compatible interstocks; assume successful compatible graft.

Sources: [source](https://www.rhs.org.uk/advice/beginners-guide/fruit-basics/rootstocks/), [source](https://open.lib.umn.edu/horticulture/part/chapter-10-grafting/)
