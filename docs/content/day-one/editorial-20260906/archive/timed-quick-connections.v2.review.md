## Q4. Why did ancient Egyptian embalmers use natron?

A. To perfume the body
B. To dye the linen
C. To dry the body **correct**
D. To glue the wrappings

NIV: Genesis 50:2–3; context GEN.50.1-6
Paid hint: Think of the small silica-gel packets packed with moisture-sensitive goods.

## Q10. For the same object still floating at rest, what increases when the water becomes less dense?

A. The upward buoyant force
B. The volume of water displaced **correct**
C. The object’s mass
D. The object’s weight

NIV: Ezekiel 47:8–9; context EZK.47.6-12
Paid hint: The water still has to support the same weight.

A floating object displaces its own weight of water. When water density falls, a greater displaced volume is needed to provide that same supporting force. Ezekiel’s vision supplies a salt-to-fresh contrast; this is a separate modern floating-object inference, not an explanation of the vision’s miraculous life-giving river.

**unapproved:** Replaces salt-word giveaway with a further density-to-displacement inference. Four physical quantities; mass and weight are distinct but correlated distractors. Assume the object remains afloat, its mass is unchanged, and local gravity is unchanged. Substantially harder than v1; unsuitable for a 2.5-second tail without a timer change.
