## 1. In traditional letterpress printing, which part of the type carries ink onto the paper?

A. The recessed grooves
B. The raised letter faces **correct**
C. The flat metal backing
D. The spaces between letters

NIV: Job 38:14 (context JOB.38.12-21).

Paid hint: Imagine which parts of an inked rubber stamp touch the paper.

Letterpress prints from raised, inked surfaces. Recessed ink belongs to intaglio; backing and spaces are not the intended printing surfaces. Job compares the emerging detail of dawn to a seal shaping clay, within God’s challenge to Job. The comparison concerns an impression, not an ancient prediction of printing.

A seal can itself be recessed, so do not claim Job establishes the raised geometry of letterpress. Test whether the stamp analogy helps beyond the seal reading.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-print), [source 2](https://printinghistory.org/timeline/).

## 2. Eratosthenes compared Sun angles in two cities. What else did he need to estimate Earth’s circumference?

A. The depth of a well
B. The distance to the Sun
C. The distance between the cities **correct**
D. The height of nearby mountains

NIV: 2 Kings 20:9-11 (context 2KI.20.4-11).

Paid hint: The angle tells him what fraction of a full circle he is measuring.

The angular difference supplies a fraction of a circle; the distance between cities supplies the corresponding arc length. A well’s depth, mountain height and Earth–Sun distance are not the missing baseline in this method. The biblical shadow moves as a divine sign to Hezekiah, not as an Earth-measurement experiment.

The passage supplies measurable shadow movement but not the city baseline. Bridge strength and hint benefit need review. Do not assert the miracle’s mechanism or a precise stadia conversion.

Sources: [source 1](https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/features/cosmic/earth_info.html), [source 2](https://spdf.gsfc.nasa.gov/pub/documents/old/websites/sunearthday.nasa.gov/2005/astronomer/act_5.htm).
