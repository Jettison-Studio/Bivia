## Q1. What is a main purpose of the side splits in running shorts?

A. To keep the waistband from slipping
B. To let the legs move more freely **correct**
C. To compress the thigh muscles
D. To support items in the pockets

Paid hint: Think about what happens to a narrow hem when you lengthen your stride.

Side splits allow the fabric to separate as the legs move. Elijah secures his cloak before running; that specific clothing adjustment supports an inference about avoiding restricted movement. The narrative attributes his feat to the Lord, not superior sportswear.

## Q2. Which test most directly measures the physical ability highlighted in Asahel’s description?

A. A timed short sprint **correct**
B. A sit-and-reach test
C. A single-leg balance test
D. A maximum-repetition push-up test

NIV: 2 Samuel 2:18–19; context 2SA.2.18-23
Paid hint: For this ability, less time over the same distance means a better result.

Asahel is described through the image of a fast-running gazelle. Timing a short sprint over a fixed distance measures that running-speed ability most directly among these options. The other tests emphasize flexibility, balance, or repeated muscular effort. The narrative is a dangerous pursuit that ends in Asahel’s death; the comparison extracts a physical description without presenting his choices as training advice.

**unapproved_repair:** Replaces historical duplicate wall-sit/isometric item. Reading identifies speed; player then selects a measurement rather than merely matching the word speed. Four genuine fitness tests. Easy two-step classification, with a clue that makes the measurement rule explicit; no measured benefit claimed. Does not infer a race distance, measured speed, athletic test, or training regime in the biblical event. No claim that a short sprint isolates peak speed from acceleration. Separate from existing Fitness running-clothing and Sports route questions.

## Q3. Holding the same dumbbell at shoulder height, which position creates the least turning effect about the shoulder?

A. Arm straight forward, palm down
B. Elbow bent, weight close to the shoulder **correct**
C. Arm straight out to the side, palm down
D. Arm straight forward, palm up

NIV: Luke 15:5; context LUK.15.3-7
Paid hint: Compare the horizontal distance between the weight and the joint.

The dumbbell produces the smallest gravitational torque when its horizontal distance from the shoulder is smallest. Its weight has not changed. Turning the palm does not change the dumbbell’s horizontal lever arm when the arm position stays the same. The shepherd places the sheep on his shoulders rather than at arm’s length; this supplies the concrete load-placement analogy. Jesus explains joy over repentance, not an exercise prescription.

**coordinator_editorial_reviewed_awaiting_verification_and_playtest:** Replaces the impossible mass/air distractors with achievable holding positions. All weights are at shoulder height; bringing the weight close reduces its horizontal lever arm. Palm orientation is a plausible but irrelevant variable for the weight’s gravitational torque. Tests external dumbbell torque only, not the total muscular effort including arm weight.

Sources: [source 1](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/9-6-forces-and-torques-in-muscles-and-joints), [source 2](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/10-6-torque)

## Q4. In a wet-floor version of this footing problem, how can shoe-tread channels help?

A. By giving the ankle more support
B. By draining liquid from beneath the sole **correct**
C. By cushioning the heel’s impact
D. By keeping the foot cooler

NIV: Psalm 94:18; context PSA.94.16-23
Paid hint: Think of the grooves on a car tire in rain.

Tread channels can provide a route for liquid to escape from the shoe-floor interface, limiting the fluid layer that reduces traction. The other choices describe different footwear functions. Psalm 94 uses slipping and support to express reliance on God amid wrongdoing; the wet floor is an explicitly imagined modern comparison, not the setting asserted by the verse.

**unapproved_repair:** Replaces held bicycle-gearing analogy to a dull axe. Four real footwear functions; tread drainage rather than cushioning is the intended distinction. Reading supplies a slipping-foot image, but it does not explain tread or specify wetness: that bridge remains the main review risk. Tire clue adds a familiar parallel without repeating the answer. Do not claim any tread guarantees traction, or generalize one material/pattern across all surfaces and contaminants. Near-context moral danger is preserved. Distinct from Challenger lateral stance width and accepted strap-pressure question.

Sources: [source 1](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/225087), [source 2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/slips/footprocure.htm), [source 3](https://www.michelinman.com/auto/auto-tips-and-advice/tires-101/tire-tread-depth)

## Q5. As an ordinary elastic exercise band is stretched farther within its working range, what usually happens to its pull?

A. It gradually weakens
B. It stays exactly constant
C. It gradually increases **correct**
D. It disappears once the band is taut

NIV: Psalm 18:34; context PSA.18.32-39
Paid hint: The material resists being pulled away from its resting shape.

Elastic band tension generally increases with elongation. The bow image associates drawing a resistant object with strength. Psalm 18 credits God with deliverance and martial strength; it does not establish an elastic-band force curve or a literal bronze-bow construction.

**author_hold:** Hold: bow-strength imagery alone does not establish increasing resistance, and the disappearance distractor is weak. PMC full-page access returned a challenge; manufacturer evidence was accessible. Do not claim linearity, universal band specifications, or measured hint benefit.
