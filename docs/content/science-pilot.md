# Science pilot — Small wonders

Editorial draft for a five-question category round. Difficulty is a proposed order, not measured player difficulty. NIV text below consists of short excerpts, not complete verses. Approved for local playtesting and installed in the local Science daily slot using scripts/seed-local-science-pilot.sql. Nothing deployed.

## 1. Room for one more

Which shape can cover a flat surface with identical regular tiles and no gaps?

A. Circle · B. Regular pentagon · C. Regular hexagon · D. Regular octagon

**Answer: C — Regular hexagon.**

**NIV excerpt:** “Eat honey, my son” — Proverbs 24:13.

**Hint connection:** Picture a honeycomb. The verse supplies a familiar example, rather than naming the shape. Regular triangles and squares also tile a plane, so neither belongs among the distractors.

**Context:** Proverbs uses honey as an image in an instruction about wisdom. This question borrows its image, not a claim that the passage teaches geometry.

Evidence: [honeycomb geometry research](https://academic.oup.com/icb/article/65/6/1678/8157891). [NIV source](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+24%3A13-14&version=NIV).

## 2. Gone into thin air

A puddle disappears on a dry afternoon without boiling. Which process explains it?

A. Condensation · B. Evaporation · C. Freezing · D. Precipitation

**Answer: B — Evaporation.**

**NIV excerpt:** “yet the sea is never full” — Ecclesiastes 1:7.

**Hint connection:** Water does not simply accumulate; think about where it goes. This is the weakest hint in the set: it evokes circulation but may not help someone distinguish the options. Test it rather than assuming relevance is usefulness.

**Context:** Ecclesiastes describes recurring patterns in nature; do not present the passage as a technical explanation of evaporation.

Evidence: [USGS water cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/water-cycle). [NIV source](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ecclesiastes+1%2CEclesiastes+1&version=NIV).

## 3. A different wavelength

What do we call the separation of white light into its component colors?

A. Absorption · B. Polarization · C. Diffraction · D. Dispersion

**Answer: D — Dispersion.**

**NIV excerpt:** “my rainbow in the clouds” — Genesis 9:13.

**Hint connection:** Visualize a rainbow as the example; retrieve the scientific term. This is an association clue, not a definition. It will help visual recall more than someone who has never learned the term.

**Context:** The rainbow is a covenant sign in the passage. The physics question does not replace or explain that theological meaning. A rainbow involves refraction and internal reflection as well as dispersion; do not ask which single process makes a rainbow.

Evidence: [NASA on color separation](https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/stargaze/Sun4spec.htm). [NIV source](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+9%3A13&version=NIV).

## 4. Opposites attract

Which type of chemical bond holds positive and negative ions together through electrical attraction?

A. Ionic · B. Covalent · C. Metallic · D. Hydrogen

**Answer: A — Ionic.**

**NIV excerpt:** “salt of the earth” — Matthew 5:13.

**Hint connection:** Table salt is the familiar example. It points toward sodium chloride without handing over the bond's name. The question still needs chemistry knowledge.

**Context:** Jesus is speaking metaphorically to his disciples. Do not turn the metaphor into a claim about chemistry or salt losing its chemical identity.

Evidence: [American Chemical Society: ionic bonding](https://www.acs.org/middleschoolchemistry/simulations/chapter4/lesson5.html). [NIV source](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matt.5.13&version=NIV).

## 5. Follow your nose

Which chemical messengers can leave a trail that guides other members of a colony toward food?

A. Antibodies · B. Enzymes · C. Pheromones · D. Pigments

**Answer: C — Pheromones.**

**NIV excerpt:** “Go to the ant” — Proverbs 6:6.

**Hint connection:** Think about an ant trail. The excerpt supplies the animal and a familiar behavior; the player still has to identify its chemical signaling mechanism. Unlike asking what a myrmecologist studies, this does not put the answer directly in the verse.

**Context:** Proverbs commends the ant as an example of diligence. The passage does not describe pheromones. Not all ant species use the same foraging behavior; the question deliberately says these messengers can guide a colony, not that every ant follows a trail.

Evidence: [research on ant trail pheromones](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6921000/). [NIV source](https://search.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+6&version=NIV).

## Playtest decision

Five questions are approved for local playtesting. This is a draft, not a measured difficulty benchmark. Start with two approachable questions, then test whether dispersion, ionic bonding and pheromones form a sensible ramp for the intended audience. The relative order of the last three may change after playtesting.

Question one is the strongest association clue. Question two is the weakest and should be replaced if players say the verse adds no help. For every question, compare solve rate before and after a hint. A relevant verse is not automatically a useful clue. No agent review can establish the difficulty curve without player evidence.
